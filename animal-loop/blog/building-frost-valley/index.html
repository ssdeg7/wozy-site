Frost Valley: Building a World Where the Road Breaks Behind You

Animal Loop's fourth world is taking shape. It has nine snowy tracks, a penguin waiting at the end, and ice that cracks every time you roll over it.

By Sebastian Salazar de Greiff · November 6, 2026 · 7 min read
01CHAPTER ONE

Every World Needs Its Own Idea

Each world in Animal Loop has something only it does. Dune Flats has switch gates that flip which way you turn. Coral Shallows has bridges and knots that cross over themselves. Fern Hollow has mud and mole tunnels. When I started on a fourth world, I knew it needed its own idea too: something that changes how you drive, not only how the track looks.

Snow was the obvious setting, and slippery ice was the obvious mechanic. But the whole game is one tap. The only thing you control is when you turn. Ice that slides you around takes that one thing away, and a one-tap game stops feeling fair the moment it feels random.

So the ice in Frost Valley doesn't change how you move. It changes the road.

02CHAPTER TWO

Ice That Remembers Every Lap

The first time you meet it, the game explains cracking ice with one card:

CRACKING ICE

Ice cracks as you roll over it and breaks into water behind you. Fall in and you lose a heart. Broken ice stays open for a lap, then freezes back as you come round again, so pick your lane each lap.

Under that card, every piece of ice has a small life of its own:

  • Every pass counts. When more than half of your animal rolls over a piece, it takes a hit. Only one hit counts per lap, so you can't break a piece by wobbling on it.
  • It breaks behind you, not under you. On its last hit, the ice gives way plate by plate, each plate only once it's fully behind you. You never drop through the ice you're on. You drop through the ice you chose to roll back onto.
  • Falling in costs a heart. If more than half of your animal ends up over open water, you splash, lose a heart, and get put back on the dry side of the road.
  • It freezes back. Broken ice stays open for a lap, then heals as you come round: the floating blocks grow and straighten into cracked plates, and the cracks fade until the ice is perfect again. It's the break, played in reverse.

Two lanes on one road

Almost every stretch of road in Frost Valley is covered in ice, split down the middle into two lanes. The pieces run end to end with no gaps, so wherever you are, there's ice on your left and ice on your right. Ride the same line every lap and you'll wear it out. The race becomes about reading which half is still good, and switching to it before your line gives way.

That's what the picture at the top shows. It's Ice Cube, drawn from the track's real data: two lanes of ice all the way round, a piece cracking under the penguin, and open water right behind it.

To make room for two lanes, Frost Valley's roads are the widest in the game. Every other world uses a road half-width between 38 and 42. Frost Valley uses 52, so each lane still fits your animal with room to steer.

The road never closes

Two lanes that can both break raise an obvious problem: what if both sides are open water at once? I didn't want any track that could trap you, so every piece of ice sits under a rule I call the freeze guard.

Whenever a piece is about to break, the game checks the road around it. If the water on one side and the water on the other would leave less than a racer's width to get through, the older water freezes back early, to cracked. Left, right, left can never close the road. A long run of water on the same side is fine, because the other lane is still there.

Some tracks even start with ice that's already cracked or broken, as if someone had raced the lap before you. Those go through the same check when the track is built, so a broken start never closes the road either.

UNDER THE HOOD

Ice turned out to be the most memory-hungry thing in the game. Every piece has its own art for every stage, from glassy to cracked to floating plates, drawn once and kept as images so nothing gets redrawn while you race. On Snowflake, the biggest track, the ice alone came to 88 MB.

So I added two tests that build every track in the game at full detail and fail if any of them goes over budget. When one does, the fix is to take out whole pairs of ice, never to make the ice look worse.

03CHAPTER THREE

Nine Tracks Shaped Like Winter

Like every world, Frost Valley has nine tracks, and this time they're named for the season.

The ladder gets harder in several ways at once:

  • Longer laps. Loop is less than a quarter of the length of Snowflake.
  • Thinner ice. On the early tracks, ice takes two hits before it breaks. By Bell, most of it breaks on the first, and on Snowflake every piece does.
  • Ice that's already gone. From Cocoa Mug on, some tracks start with stretches already cracked or open.
  • Slower healing. On Snowflake, broken ice stays open for two laps instead of one.
  • Ice caves. The first four tracks have none. From Santa's Sack on, every track has one, a shortcut through the snow.
  • Switch gates. The first three tracks have none. Star Knot and Snowflake have ten each.

The two knots, Ribbon Knot and Star Knot, are the odd ones out. The road near a crossing has to stay clear of ice, and on a knot most of the road is near a crossing. So they test something different: less ice to read, more crossings to thread.

Laying out 236 pieces of ice

Across the nine tracks, Frost Valley has 236 pieces of ice. Placing all of them by hand wasn't realistic, so I wrote a small script that lays the two lanes along every stretch of road where ice is allowed (clear of the start line, the switch gates, the ice caves and the crossings) and then I tuned each track by hand.

For that, the Level Creator got a new ICE tool. I can drag a piece's ends, keep its partner in the other lane lined up, choose whether it starts intact, cracked or broken, and get checks that stop a play-test if a piece breaks the rules.

04CHAPTER FOUR

Snow Is Only White at Noon

The hard part of drawing a snow world is that white is boring, and real snow is almost never just white. It takes the color of the sky. So Frost Valley's snow follows the game's day/night cycle: pink at dawn, white only at noon, lilac at dusk and navy at night. The one warm color that holds all day is the orange of the track's border markers.

At night, something else happens. The wind streaks across the snowfield turn into aurora curtains, green with a magenta fringe, and the lit side of every snowbank glows aurora green. For most of its development this world was called Aurora Drift. The name changed. The aurora stayed.

Around the tracks you'll find snow firs, igloos, crevasses (some with rope bridges across them) and snow angels pressed into the snow. Like every world, each track's decorations are seeded from the track itself, so a crevasse is always turned, mirrored and sized the same way on the same track.

Frost Valley gets its own music, too. The music in Animal Loop is played live as you race, and each world has its own instruments. Here, a music box carries the melody over a warm pad, with an acoustic bass underneath.

05CHAPTER FIVE

A Penguin at the Finish

Every world ends in a Grand Prix against its rival animal: the Armadillo in Dune Flats, the Puffer in Coral Shallows, the Ladybug in Fern Hollow. Frost Valley's rival is the Penguin, a toboggan racer. Beat it across all nine tracks and it joins your roster.

The Penguin is one of the newest racers, and it's drawn a little differently from the original animals. Instead of hand-coding every pose, its shapes come straight from its design sheet and the game replays them, so the penguin you race looks exactly like the one in the drawing.

CLOSING

What's Still Left

Frost Valley isn't in the game yet. Right now it lives behind a switch in my development builds. Before it ships, it still needs its Game Center leaderboard and achievement, its own app icon, and more time on the ice ladder, because I want the step from Fern Hollow to Frost Valley to feel like the next step, not a wall.

If you want to know the moment it lands, subscribe to the dev diary or follow @wozyofficial on X. And if you have thoughts on the ice, I'd love to hear them. Send me a message.
